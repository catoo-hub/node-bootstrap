On keeping a slow inbox

12 min read · April 2026

For most of my career I treated email as a queue. Each message was a task to be processed, dispositioned, or deleted. The discipline felt productive. The reality was that I was performing inbox hygiene while the work I was actually paid to do drifted past, untouched.

This year I started doing something different. I check email twice a day — once around 11:00, after the morning's deep work, and once around 17:00, before stopping for the evening. The rest of the day, the application is closed.

The point isn't to ignore the inbox. The point is to spend the day building, not triaging.

Predictably, almost nothing has actually broken. The handful of messages that needed an urgent reply found me by other means — direct message, a hallway tap, an actual phone call. The slow inbox didn't make me unavailable; it made me available only to people who had a real reason to interrupt.

I'm not arguing this works for every role. Customer support, sales, and on-call engineers have different constraints. But for the kind of work that compounds — writing, designing, planning — the cost of partial attention is almost always greater than the cost of a delayed reply.
